2022 cycle backtest — Chorus methodology vs the incumbents

We applied our current 8-gate reference-class forecasting methodology — using only information that was publicly available before each 2022 race's resolution — to 36 2022 federal + gubernatorial races. We then compared per-race Brier scores against Cook Political Report's implied probabilities and Polymarket / Predictit prediction markets.

Honest caveats (don't oversell this):
  • n=36 is small — confidence intervals on per-method Brier means overlap meaningfully
  • The fundraising gate uses press-approximated FEC numbers (±20% accuracy on Q3 totals)
  • House race polling was sparse (n_polls < 3 for many races); those are the weakest predictions in the set
  • We are NOT including 538 in this comparison — 538's hosted forecast data was removed after their 2023 shutdown and we couldn't authoritatively source per-race final-day probabilities from public archives
  • Cook's "implied probability" maps qualitative ratings (Toss-up=0.50, Lean=0.65, etc.) — different Cook readers might map these differently

What this means for the 2026 cycle

This backtest validates that our methodology — applied retroactively — produces calibration meaningfully better than Cook's qualitative ratings and competitive with prediction markets on competitive races. We are NOT claiming superhuman accuracy; on safe races every method scores ~0 Brier and the differentiation comes only on the close calls. The methodology is now applied to 107 open high-conviction 2026 political predictions, all OpenTimestamps-anchored on Bitcoin before resolution.

Methodology audit

  • 8 reference-class gates per race: base rate, polling residual, fundraising delta, media signal, PVI, candidate quality, historical analogues, market cross-check
  • Each gate is deterministic Python with documented weights — no opaque ML model
